# Revised 36-second cut

A persistent deterministic Three.js stage carries the product. Three overlay compositions carry the short copy and captured proof. They are independently authored per Hyperframes' frame-worker workflow, then assembled and verified by the root agent.

## Video direction

Forest, ivory and sage from the real app. Local Arial-compatible font, registered under a private alias; mono only for source or build identifiers. Use large, short copy and move it with one long-tail ease family. The product should dominate most frames. No repeated full-page screenshot cards, decorative UI, invented numbers, infinite drift or full-screen fades. Macro views deliberately crop the keyboard. Listening and final URL receive explicit held reads.

## Act one: touch and identity, 0–10

0–2.4: camera tracks across F/G/H and the spacebar. Four recorded down/up events motivate actual key travel. No headline covers the material.
2.4–5.3: camera pulls back and rises to reveal the complete board on the right. 'Make it' then 'yours.' resolves on the clear left field. Product movement, then readable hold through 6.
6–10: the camera orbits; color changes travel across rows from Forest Line to Blush to Midnight and back. Replace only the preset name on the corresponding cue. 'Your colors.' is the concise explanatory line. No page screenshots.

## Act two: construction and hearing, 10–23

10–14.6: camera orbits around separating keycaps, individual switch housings, perforated plate and PCB. 'Every layer.' on the left; labels reveal in construction order and point toward the visible layers without crossing the board.
14.6–19: reassemble the keyboard while a native-resolution crop of actual component choices enters in the left field. 'Choose your parts.' moves into position above it. Read selected real part names. These captures prove the app, while the right-hand study remains a representative model.
19–23: cut on the keypress to a macro. 'Hear the difference.' and 'Gateron Ink Black · recorded reference' remain in the upper negative space. The waveform uses precomputed amplitudes from the actual recording. Key travel uses those exact event times. The score drops to let the transients lead.

## Act three: play and invitation, 23–36

23–27: real Monkeytype footage fills a broad widget across the upper-middle canvas. The keyboard sits beneath it; key travel follows actual captured event timestamps. 'Find your rhythm.' is a short top heading. No invented speed or accuracy result.
27–31: the widget recedes; the camera and preset changes continue into a full-board beauty shot. 'A build that feels like you.' appears in two timed fragments.
31–36: the camera settles, 'keyconf' and 'Make it yours.' resolve on the left. Hold a readable actual launch URL through the last frame. End on the product, not on black.
