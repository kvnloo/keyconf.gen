# Creator workflow research for Keyconf

Researched September 6, 2026. This is a representative sample of eight keyboard creators and businesses, selected for different observable workflows. It is not a ranking of the biggest channels. Public subscriber counts returned by search were inconsistent, including different counts for the same video, so this report does not use them.

Keyconf's clearest starting point is a shareable keyboard build page that can become a client brief. The same page can connect a creator's photos and sound test to its exact parts, discussion, and a request for a similar build. Public evidence supports this connection. Willingness to move community activity or pay for software remains untested.

## What the workflows reveal

The common public object is the individual build. Taeha's projects join finished photos, collaborators, a build process and a typing recording. Glarses and alexotos put component recipes in video descriptions. Mod Musings connects preferred configurations to sound demonstrations. A build page can preserve these relationships and send people back to the original content. This is an inference about product utility, not a claim that creators have requested it.

The strongest direct evidence of operational friction is parts coordination. Lightning explicitly says following numerous client orders and storing them over long periods exceeds his capacity. He generally asks clients to gather the parts, allows limited exceptions and handles tailored sourcing separately. Alexotos prefers a consolidated package to avoid confusion, limits sourcing to available stock with advance payment, and records damage or PCB problems on receipt. These are stated constraints, not guessed complaints. [Lightning's sourcing FAQ](https://www.lightningkeyboards.com/commissions), [alexotos's service terms](https://www.alexotos.com/keyboard-build-service/).

Feedback has several different audiences. Alexotos's request form and follow-up contact support private specifications and exceptions; his receipt checks can require the customer's response. Lightning separates assembly from promotional deliverables. Hipyo's [video description](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sf049gG30is) asks viewers for future content ideas and links community channels. Public audience conversation, private client approval and a vendor's review request should therefore have different visibility and status. Their private conversations and actual revision counts were not accessible.

Product availability needs dates. Mod Musings keeps an explicit update above an older drop description. Glarses uses restock and dispatch notifications. Hipyo's product page returned both in-stock and backorder text in the extraction, so no live inventory claim should be inferred from that page. A saved creator recommendation should retain its source and check date; checkout and live stock can stay with the vendor. [Encore history](https://mod-musings.squarespace.com/modmusings-encore-keyboard), [Glarses FAQ](https://glarses.com/pages/faq-1), [Hipyo product page](https://hipyo.co/products/pikarar-x-hipyo-tech-pbt-keycaps).

## Minimum product priorities

These priorities are recommendations inferred from the reviewed workflows.

1. Publish a build. Give it a photo, author, exact component recipe, physical layout, modifications, source links and an optional sound or build-video link. Allow planned and completed builds. Put recording context beside audio; do not imply that two recordings establish a controlled sound comparison.
2. Discuss and adapt that build. Attach questions to the build or a component. Let someone save it or create their own version with visible credit to the source. A creator should be able to share one durable URL from their existing channels.
3. Turn a build into a private request. Let the requester describe the intended use, budget, visual references and changes. Track client-supplied versus builder-sourced parts and whether each is still needed or received. Include private/public preference and requested media. A request is pending until the builder accepts it.

Start with those three connected actions. Creator profiles can simply collect builds, commission availability and existing social/shop links. Keep purchases, video hosting and general chat on the services creators already use. Marketplace checkout, manufacturing schedules, comprehensive inventory, follower feeds and sponsorship management would add substantial scope before demand is established.

For a pilot, ask several consenting builders to use a shared build brief for real requests. Measure missing-information follow-ups, time to an accepted specification, and whether viewers reuse the linked build. Separately test whether viewers return to ask or answer useful build questions. No outreach was performed for this research.

## Evidence limits

- Sources were reviewed on September 6, 2026. Undated commission pages establish only their displayed status on that date. Historical portfolio entries establish past work, not current clients or availability.
- Website text, public channel descriptions, public link hubs and product pages were accessible. Full YouTube pages sometimes failed or were throttled; those observations use the creator-authored descriptions returned by search. Videos were not watched end to end. Instagram and Discord conversations, customer inboxes, analytics and payment records were not accessed.
- No private pain point, creator endorsement, willingness to pay, market size or ranking is claimed. Taeha's esports portfolio and Glarses's creator collaborations are verified examples, not evidence that all listed creators build for professionals.
- Seven rows have publicly accessible service, shop or content evidence beyond the older illustrative projects. :3ildcat is retained with a specific current-access limitation. This sample favors inspectable workflows over a claim of exhaustive creator coverage.
